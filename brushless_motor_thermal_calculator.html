Launch optimizer

The Current cap above is the hard post-BEMF ceiling. With voltage search enabled, the optimizer compares 16.8 V (4S), 21.0 V (5S), and 25.2 V (6S), plus the exact Pack voltage slider value if it differs. It automatically varies starting current, transition RPM, finishing current, and (for 3-stage profiles) a second transition/current. Current is allowed to rise or fall — taper is not assumed. It now also tests drag-compensated constant acceleration, an adaptive deadline-tracking controller, unrestricted 8-point freeform, monotonic 8-point freeform, smooth-monotonic 8-point freeform, and a C¹ monotone cubic-spline current-vs-RPM profile. The smooth variant uses smoothness only as a tie-break when heat is within the configured tiny heat window, so it does not deliberately trade meaningful thermal efficiency for appearance. Startup current is now independent of the post-BEMF current ceiling; when startup optimization is enabled, the search also varies startup start current, current at BEMF lock, and handoff-ramp duration. The optional slew limiter is applied to post-BEMF current commands so step/freeform solutions cannot exploit instantaneous current changes. A common speed-PI catch can also cap every strategy near target RPM. The PI handoff is now truly bumpless: when the catch first arms, a temporary tracking bias is initialized so the PI output exactly matches the optimized launch current, then that bias decays to zero as RPM error closes. Integral action remains separate and small. When automatic PI tuning is enabled, the tool searches catch RPM, Kp, Ki, and integral-band settings only on the best trajectory candidates rather than multiplying the entire 10k-profile search. The simulator continues beyond first target crossing to report overshoot and settling behavior. Whole-pack mode still finds (1) one fixed profile that must meet the deadline across the discharge range and (2) a voltage-aware lookup-table strategy that can choose a different optimized profile from measured pre-launch pack voltage.

Thermal network

Model assumptions

  • Ideal commutation, averaged BLDC/DC-equivalent model; no field weakening, saturation, desync, or commutation timing error.
  • Battery sag is represented by one lumped source resistance. Under PWM, battery current is approximated as D × motor current.
  • Before usable BEMF is available, current follows a separate open-loop startup ramp in amperes; it is no longer tied to a percentage of the post-BEMF current ceiling. The optimizer can vary startup start current, current at BEMF lock, and BEMF handoff-ramp duration around the user-selected baseline values.
  • After BEMF lock, optimized profiles blend from the startup current ceiling into the selected closed-loop profile over the modeled handoff interval. When enabled, a symmetric current-command slew-rate limit is then applied to all post-BEMF controlled strategies. The absolute full-bus physical floor intentionally bypasses that post-BEMF slew limit.
  • Constant-current control after BEMF lock is treated as an ideal current loop. If the required duty exceeds 100%, the motor becomes voltage-limited.
  • The optimizer is allowed to vary current in both directions with RPM. It searches constant, ramp, 2-stage, and 3-stage profiles; a “taper” is only selected if the model actually prefers one.
  • When voltage search is enabled, 4S/5S/6S full-charge voltage is treated as an optimizer design choice. Pack/wiring resistance remains fixed, so this compares voltage while holding pack stiffness constant unless you change that slider yourself.
  • The “unoptimized” comparison is defined as the same pack voltage and same pre-BEMF startup followed by 100% duty/full-send after BEMF. This gives a clear apples-to-apples baseline for how much heat the optimized current profile saves.
  • The battery-degradation plots now compare three cases: the original full-charge-tuned profile held fixed, one static robust profile optimized to satisfy all sampled voltages, and a voltage-aware lookup-table strategy that selects a precomputed profile from measured pre-launch pack voltage.
  • The voltage-aware LUT search explicitly includes the final fixed robust whole-pack winner as a candidate at every voltage bin and re-checks that dominance at the final 0.10 ms simulation timestep. The dense 0.05 V/cell sweep may also fall back to the fixed robust profile between LUT bins. Therefore the reported dynamic LUT cannot legitimately have a shorter feasible voltage range, or higher optimized heat where the fixed robust solution is a legal choice.
  • The whole-pack objective hard-constrains time-to-target at seven optimizer bins from 4.20 to 3.30 V/cell and minimizes equal-bin average motor heat. Equal voltage-bin weighting is a design approximation, not a detailed LiPo state-of-charge probability model.
  • The displayed strategy cutoff voltage is taken from a denser 0.05 V/cell sweep. When a strategy fails below that cutoff, the model is saying that the launch misses the deadline there even with its best searched current shaping.
  • “Sum heat” is the simple sum of motor heat over the feasible dense-sweep sample points, included as a comparative stress metric rather than literal energy over one real battery discharge cycle.
  • The strategy search now also includes a drag-compensated constant-acceleration family, an adaptive deadline tracker, and an 8-point freeform current-vs-RPM family. The freeform profile is refined by deterministic coordinate descent around the best conventional result rather than brute-forcing every possible 8-dimensional combination.
  • The deadline tracker is a simulated feedback law: after BEMF it recomputes the average acceleration still required to reach target by a chosen finish time, adds the modeled drag-current term, and clamps the result to the current ceiling / available bus voltage.
  • The 8-point freeform family uses fixed RPM fractions and linearly interpolated current control points. Its purpose is to test whether the older constant/linear/staged families were artificially constraining the optimum.
  • Three additional 8-point families are searched: monotonic non-decreasing current, smooth-monotonic current, and a monotone cubic spline. The spline uses shape-preserving cubic Hermite interpolation, including zero slope outside the first and last knots, so its commanded current is continuous with continuous slope and cannot overshoot its neighboring control points.
  • The optional speed PI layer is common to every controlled strategy and acts as a late outer current ceiling near target RPM: current command = min(optimized profile, speed-PI demand, hardware ceiling). The handoff is bumpless: at first catch activation a temporary tracking bias is initialized so PI demand exactly equals the optimized profile current, then that bias decays to zero with closing positive RPM error. Integral action is a separate small correction, disabled until the error is inside the configured integral band and protected by conditional integration / anti-windup.
  • When automatic PI tuning is enabled, the broad ~10k trajectory search runs without PI or post-hit settling. A second-stage staged PI search is then run only on a small set of the strongest family finalists (plus the smooth-monotonic candidate). It uses a compact coarse grid followed by local coordinate refinement for catch RPM, Kp, Ki, and integral band. This keeps PI from distorting trajectory selection and removes thousands of unnecessary settling simulations.
  • Controlled-profile simulations continue beyond the first 32k crossing for the configured settling window. Overshoot above the configured limit makes the candidate infeasible. A controller is not considered settled until it remains continuously inside the settling band for the configured dwell time; any excursion resets the dwell timer. Full-bus physical-floor results remain a first-crossing reference and do not use PI.
  • PI auto-tuning now receives a trajectory timing-margin seed bank instead of only the lowest-heat trajectory sitting at the launch deadline. For a 150 ms target, finalists are deliberately retained around roughly 138, 142, 145, 147, 149, and 150 ms, plus fastest and firmware-friendly smooth/monotonic trajectories. This lets the optimizer spend a few milliseconds on the final speed catch without falsely declaring higher-voltage packs infeasible.
  • Across the four 8-point freeform families, the final recommendation prefers the spline, then smooth-monotonic, monotonic, and unrestricted implementations only when predicted motor peak is within 0.10 °C and heat is within the configured smooth heat-tie window. Within that thermal tie, lower pack energy is preferred before implementation smoothness. Meaningful thermal improvements always override these tie-breakers.
  • The optimizer's “absolute time floor” uses the same pre-BEMF startup, followed by 100% duty after BEMF. If that floor misses the deadline, no current-profile algorithm can meet the deadline without changing another physical/control assumption.
  • The thermal motor node is an effective short-timescale motor temperature, not a guaranteed winding hot-spot temperature.
  • Two identical motors are assumed. The plotted motor temperature is one representative motor; the plate receives heat from both.
  • Each shot deposits motor copper heat essentially instantaneously relative to the multi-second thermal time constants.

How to read it

The reported time-to-target starts at t = 0 and therefore includes the pre-BEMF open-loop startup interval and modeled BEMF handoff ramp. Startup current is independent of the post-BEMF ceiling. After BEMF lock, controlled strategies may also be constrained by the selected current slew-rate limit; the absolute full-bus floor remains the physical no-slew lower bound. The taper strategy intentionally gives away some acceleration near target speed in exchange for lower late-shot current and usually lower heat. Full voltage is included as an upper-performance / upper-stress reference, not as a recommendation. The thermal plot is mainly useful for comparing strategies and sensitivity to Rθ, thermal mass, airflow, and shot cadence.
